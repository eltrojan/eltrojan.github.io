What just happened

You received a message that looked legitimate, followed its link, and entered your information on a fake page controlled by this test. That single submission is all an attacker needs to begin a breach — no malware required.

How to detect phishing next time

  1. 1
    Inspect the sender's real address
    Don't trust the display name. Expand the actual email address and look for lookalikes like micros0ft-support.com or a public domain (@gmail.com) pretending to be your company.
  2. 2
    Hover before you click
    Point at a link (don't click) and read the real destination. If the domain isn't the one you expect, stop.
  3. 3
    Distrust urgency, threats, rewards and exclusivity
    "Act now", "account suspended", "you've been selected", "limited seats" — manufactured pressure is designed to rush you past your judgment.
  4. 4
    Never sign in through an email link
    Reach portals and HR tools from your own bookmarks or by typing the address yourself.
  5. 5
    Check the address bar before you type anything
    Confirm the exact domain and the padlock. https:// alone does not mean a site is safe.
  6. 6
    Watch for odd details
    Generic greetings ("Dear user"), spelling slips, mismatched dates, or a request that doesn't fit your role.
  7. 7
    Verify unexpected requests out-of-band
    Asked for credentials, payment or data? Confirm through a known phone number or chat — never by replying.
  8. 8
    When in doubt, report it
    Reporting a suspicious email protects everyone — always better than clicking to "just check".
